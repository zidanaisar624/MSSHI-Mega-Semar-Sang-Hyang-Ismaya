# brainstorming-research-ideas

Guides researchers through 10 structured ideation frameworks (Problem-First vs Solution-First, Abstraction Ladder, Tension Hunting, Cross-Pollination, What Changed, Boundary Probing, Simplicity Test, Stakeholder Rotation, Composition/Decomposition, Explain-It Test).

## Workflow

1. State the problem, field, population or dataset, constraints and known results. Ask which outcomes would count as a useful contribution.
2. Choose two or three lenses from problem/solution-first, abstraction ladder, tension hunting, cross-pollination, What Changed, boundary probing, simplicity, stakeholder rotation, composition/decomposition and Explain-It Test.
3. Generate distinct candidates. Specify a research question, plausible method, required evidence, evaluation and main feasibility risk for each.
4. Rank candidates by contribution, feasibility and evidence gap. Propose one cheap first check and mark novelty as unverified until literature is checked.
