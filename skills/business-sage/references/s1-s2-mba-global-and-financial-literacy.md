# S1 (BBA) to S2 (MBA) Global Curriculum & Financial Literacy Reference

Use this reference when executing **Pillar 1** of `business-sage`. It synthesizes the 16-Unit Financial Literacy curriculum (Khan Academy / Tina Huang) with the core curricula of Harvard Business School (HBS), Wharton, Stanford GSB, MIT Sloan, INSEAD, and Prof. Aswath Damodaran (NYU Stern).

## 1. Foundational Financial Literacy (16-Unit Personal & Founder Finance Audit)

Before scaling a company, audit the founder's or household's financial baseline:

1. **After-Tax Budgeting & The 50/30/20 Rule**:
   - Always calculate budgets from **Net After-Tax Income**:
     - **50% Needs**: Housing, basic groceries, utilities, minimum debt payments, essential transport, basic insurance.
     - **30% Wants**: Dining out, entertainment, discretionary upgrades.
     - **20% Savings & Investments**: Emergency fund first, then high-APR debt elimination, then long-term compounding assets.
   - **Per-Unit Pricing Audit**: Evaluate every recurring purchase by cost per unit or cost per usage cycle ($	ext{Cost per Cycle} = rac{	ext{Total Price}}{	ext{Total Uses}}$). In business, this directly translates into **Activity-Based Costing (ABC)** and **Unit COGS**.

2. **Emergency Fund, Net Worth & SMART Financial Goals**:
   - **Emergency Runway**: Maintain **3 to 6 months of essential living/operating expenses** in liquid, low-risk accounts (high-yield savings, money market, or LPS-insured bank accounts in Indonesia) before locking capital into illiquid ventures.
   - **Net Worth Formula**: $	ext{Net Worth} = \sum 	ext{Assets} - \sum 	ext{Liabilities}$.
   - **Time-Horizon Allocation**:
     - *Short-Term (<1 year)*: Cash / Money Market (preserve principal).
     - *Medium-Term (1-5 years)*: Fixed-term deposits / CDs / Government Bonds (SBN/ORI/Sukuk Ritel).
     - *Long-Term (>5 years)*: Diversified Low-Cost Index Funds (e.g., S&P 500 historical ~10% nominal / ~6-7% real return), productive business equity, or cash-flowing real estate.

3. **Credit Mechanics, Loans, Debt & Schumer Box Audit**:
   - **5 Credit Score Factors (FICO 300-850 / SLIK OJK Equivalent)**:
     1. *Payment History (35%)*: Never miss a due date.
     2. *Credit Utilization (30%)*: $rac{	ext{Current Balance}}{	ext{Total Credit Limit}}$ (keep below 10-30%).
     3. *Length of Credit History (15%)*.
     4. *Credit Mix (10%)*: Revolving vs Installment.
     5. *New Credit / Hard Inquiries (10%)*: Avoid multiple hard pulls in a short window.
   - **Good Debt vs Bad Debt**:
     - *Good Debt*: Borrowed capital that acquires an asset or cashflow stream whose **After-Tax ROIC exceeds the loan's APR** ($	ext{ROIC}_{	ext{after-tax}} > 	ext{APR}$).
     - *Bad Debt*: High-APR revolving credit card debt (`20%-28.99%+ APR`) or payday/illegal online loans (`300%-800% APR`) used to fund depreciating consumption.
   - **Debt Payoff Algorithms**:
     - *Avalanche (High-Rate Approach)*: Pay minimums on all debts, direct 100% of surplus cash to the debt with the **highest APR** (mathematically minimizes total interest paid).
     - *Snowball Method*: Direct surplus cash to the **smallest balance first** (maximizes psychological momentum and behavioral adherence).

4. **Compound Interest, Inflation & Rent-vs-Buy Quantitative Model**:
   - **Compound Growth**: $FV = PV 	imes \left(1 + rac{r}{m}ight)^{m \cdot t} + PMT 	imes rac{\left(1 + rac{r}{m}ight)^{m \cdot t} - 1}{r / m}$. Starting 10 years earlier with half the monthly contribution outperforms starting late with double the contribution.
   - **Real Purchasing Power Return**: $r_{	ext{real}} = rac{1 + r_{	ext{nominal}}}{1 + \pi_{	ext{inflation}}} - 1 pprox r_{	ext{nominal}} - \pi_{	ext{inflation}}$.
   - **Rent vs Buy Decision Equation**:
     $$	ext{Annual Cost of Buying} = (	ext{Mortgage Interest} - 	ext{Tax Deduction Benefit}) + 	ext{Property Tax} + 	ext{Maintenance} + (	ext{Down Payment} 	imes r_{	ext{opportunity}})$$
     Compare against $	ext{Annual Rent}$. For early-stage businesses, leasing equipment/space preserves working capital for high-ROIC inventory turnover.

## 2. S1-S2 MBA Financial Accounting & Control (FRC)

1. **The 3 Linked Financial Statements**:
   - **Income Statement (Laporan Laba Rugi)**: $	ext{Revenue} - 	ext{COGS} = 	ext{Gross Profit}$; $	ext{Gross Profit} - 	ext{OpEx} = 	ext{EBITDA}$; $	ext{EBITDA} - 	ext{D\&A} = 	ext{EBIT (Operating Income)}$; $	ext{EBIT} - 	ext{Interest} - 	ext{Taxes} = 	ext{Net Income}$.
   - **Balance Sheet (Neraca)**: $	ext{Assets} = 	ext{Liabilities} + 	ext{Shareholders' Equity}$ (must balance to 0.00).
   - **Cash Flow Statement (Arus Kas)**: $\Delta 	ext{Cash} = 	ext{CFO (Operating)} + 	ext{CFI (Investing)} + 	ext{CFF (Financing)}$. Profit is accounting opinion; cash flow is fact.

2. **Working Capital & Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)**:
   $$	ext{DIO} = rac{	ext{Inventory}}{	ext{COGS}} 	imes 365, \quad 	ext{DSO} = rac{	ext{Accounts Receivable}}{	ext{Total Credit Sales}} 	imes 365, \quad 	ext{DPO} = rac{	ext{Accounts Payable}}{	ext{COGS}} 	imes 365$$
   $$	ext{CCC} = 	ext{DIO} + 	ext{DSO} - 	ext{DPO}$$
   - **Negative CCC ($	ext{CCC} < 0$)**: The holy grail of bootstrapping. Customers pay upfront ($	ext{DSO} pprox 0$), inventory turns fast ($	ext{DIO}$ low), and suppliers grant credit terms ($	ext{DPO}$ high), meaning growth generates cash rather than consuming it.

## 3. Corporate Finance & Intrinsic Valuation (Damodaran / Wharton / HBS)

1. **Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) & Equity (FCFE)**:
   $$	ext{FCFF} = 	ext{EBIT}(1 - t) + 	ext{D\&A} - 	ext{CapEx} - \Delta 	ext{Non-Cash Working Capital}$$
   $$	ext{FCFE} = 	ext{Net Income} + 	ext{D\&A} - 	ext{CapEx} - \Delta 	ext{Non-Cash WC} + (	ext{New Debt Issued} - 	ext{Debt Repaid})$$

2. **Cost of Capital (WACC & CAPM with Country Risk)**:
   $$R_e = R_f + eta 	imes (	ext{ERP}_{	ext{mature}} + 	ext{CRP}_{	ext{country}})$$
   $$	ext{WACC} = \left(rac{E}{E + D}ight) R_e + \left(rac{D}{E + D}ight) R_d (1 - t)$$

3. **Intrinsic DCF Valuation & Terminal Value**:
   $$	ext{Terminal Value (TV)}_n = rac{	ext{FCFF}_n 	imes (1 + g)}{	ext{WACC} - g} \quad (	ext{where } g \le 	ext{nominal GDP growth})$$
   $$	ext{Enterprise Value (EV)} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} rac{	ext{FCFF}_t}{(1 + 	ext{WACC})^t} + rac{	ext{TV}_n}{(1 + 	ext{WACC})^n}$$
   $$	ext{Equity Value} = 	ext{Enterprise Value} + 	ext{Cash} - 	ext{Total Debt}$$

4. **Economic Value Creation (ROIC vs WACC)**:
   $$	ext{ROIC} = rac{	ext{NOPAT}}{	ext{Invested Capital}} = rac{	ext{EBIT}(1 - t)}{	ext{Total Debt} + 	ext{Total Equity} - 	ext{Non-Operating Cash}}$$
   - If $	ext{ROIC} > 	ext{WACC}$, growth creates value. If $	ext{ROIC} < 	ext{WACC}$, rapid growth destroys value faster.

## 4. Startup, SaaS & Retail Unit Economics

## 5. Strategic Management, Operations & Decision Science

- **Porter's 5 Forces**: Supplier Power, Buyer Power, Threat of Substitutes, Threat of New Entrants, and Competitive Rivalry (determines industry structural ROIC ceiling).
- **VRIO Moat Audit**: Is the resource *Valuable, Rare, Inimitable,* and *Organized to capture value*?
- **Blue Ocean ERRC Grid**: *Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create* to break the value-cost trade-off.
- **Little's Law (Operations)**: $L = \lambda 	imes W$ (*Average Inventory / WIP = Throughput Rate $	imes$ Flow Time*). To cut customer wait time $W$ without slowing throughput $\lambda$, reduce work-in-progress $L$.
- **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)**: $Q^* = \sqrt{rac{2 \cdot D \cdot S}{H}}$ (where $D$ = annual demand, $S$ = order setup cost, $H$ = annual holding cost per unit).
